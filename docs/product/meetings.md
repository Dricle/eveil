# Booking a call from a reply

When somebody answers that they are interested, the next step is a call. Eveil helps you offer times and send the invite, but you always send the words yourself: no reply ever goes to a prospect on its own, whatever the [autonomy level](/product/autonomy).

## Set your meeting hours once

**Settings → Meetings** holds:

- **Time zone.** The zone your hours are in. It starts from your browser's.
- **A call lasts.** 15 minutes to an hour and a half.
- **Hours.** One row per window, for example Tuesday 10:00 to 12:00 and Thursday 14:00 to 16:00. Add two rows for a split day.
- **Where.** A video link, a phone number or an address. It goes into the calendar invite, never into the mail itself.

No calendar is connected. These hours are all Eveil knows about when you are free, so leave out the ones you are never available. Until the hours are set, the inbox shows a link to this page instead of the buttons below.

## Propose times

Open a conversation in the inbox and click **Propose times**. Eveil picks three free times from your hours, one per day, starting tomorrow, and skips any time a call is already booked. It writes a short answer offering them, in the language of their reply and following the email box of **Settings → AI instructions**, and puts it in the reply box.

Nothing is sent. Read it, change what you like, and send it like any other reply. Each draft is one AI call.

## Send the invite

When they pick a time, click **Attach an invite**. Choose one of the times you offered, or type another, then write your answer and send. The reply goes from the same mailbox, in the same thread, with a calendar invite attached: their calendar offers to add it, and their answer comes back to your mailbox.

The call then shows on the conversation, and counts in **Meetings booked** on the dashboard.

Cold mail never carries an invite. It only ever goes with a reply you wrote to somebody who already answered.
